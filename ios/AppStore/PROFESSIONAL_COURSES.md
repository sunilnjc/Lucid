# Role-specific professional courses

## Curriculum

Lucid now has an authored 90-word/phrase, 30-lesson, six-chapter path for each of its eight roles: Finance & Accounting; Project & Product; Technology & Engineering; Sales & Business Development; People & Human Resources; Consulting & Strategy; Operations & Supply Chain; and Healthcare.

The catalogue contains 472 distinct entries. Shared vocabulary occupies a slot in more than one course; 720 course slots does not mean 720 different terms. All 143 original word IDs and terms remain present, so existing bookmarks, introduced words, and scheduled reviews retain their identities. Finance's existing path and lesson IDs are also retained.

Each lesson includes three words, an objective, a workplace scenario, and a model response. Each word has collocations, an example, usage guidance, a common-mistake warning, and a practice prompt. Shared terms use role-specific contexts, including different grammatical senses where necessary. A lesson can span multiple days at a one- or two-word daily pace: 30 lessons is not a promise of completion in 30 calendar days.

Progression moves from concrete updates and clarification toward evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, recommendations, and leadership communication. Difficulty tags are editorial guides, not a CEFR certification. Healthcare material teaches communication only; it is not clinically validated and does not provide clinical decision-making training.

## Optional starting-point check

On iOS, open Today or the professional path and select **Find my starting point**. There are six role-specific recognition questions and a **Not sure** option. Closing the sheet preserves submitted answers on this device.

- Six correct answers, including both foundation questions: suggest chapter five.
- Four or five correct answers, including both foundation questions: suggest chapter three.
- Otherwise: suggest chapter one.

The user must explicitly apply a suggestion and may always start at chapter one. This is a tentative course starting point, not a diagnosis of English proficiency, grammar, speaking ability, or job seniority. Onboarding calls seniority **career stage** and explains the distinction.

Earlier chapters remain accessible and return after the later chapters. Moving the starting point does not introduce words, mark them mastered, earn XP, or alter review intervals. A plan with saved work stays intact; the new starting point then applies to the next newly generated daily plan. An untouched plan can update immediately. Other roles' daily-plan caches are preserved.

## Persistence and privacy

Submitted answers save durably before advancing. Failed writes remain visible and retryable. Drafts are scoped to the current guest/account and role, and are excluded from cloud uploads. An applied starting-point preference can sync with existing consented account data.

Drafts carry a deterministic question-content signature so an app update cannot silently reinterpret old answer indexes. Updated questions restart only the unfinished check, not history or the selected starting point. Backup import prefers a valid incoming draft when the local one is empty, stale, or absent.

## Release limits and next priorities

The native course and starting-check flow are the first delivery. The website shares the catalogue, but does not yet have complete native-course/starting-check UI parity. This work does not publish a website or install a new build on a user's iPhone by itself.

Professional editorial review and a small learner pilot are still needed before public release, especially for healthcare. Automated tests cannot establish real-world usefulness, learning efficacy, or clinical validity. The subsequent build 2 now includes tap-first daily practice with optional writing/speaking and clear pause/resume, plus the new logo and iOS icon. See DAILY_PRACTICE.md, BRAND.md and the latest QA_REPORT.md delivery evidence.
